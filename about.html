About

A project held by a life

One Long Thread brings archival research, sound and collaborative composition to bear on the history and present of gynaecological racism.

Anarcha

Where did she go?

Between 1845 and 1849, in Montgomery, Alabama, Dr. J. Marion Sims performed repeated experimental operations on enslaved women. Anarcha was one of them. By Sims’s own account, she underwent around thirty operations.

She was later declared “cured”. We use that word deliberately and in quotation marks: there is little evidence about what her life was like afterwards, and almost nothing in her own voice.

One Long Thread begins from that silence. It does not try to fill it with certainty. It listens to it, composes with it, and asks what the archive’s gaps can teach us about how Black women’s bodies and accounts are still heard, or not heard, in care today.

Where this work began

This work began with a story: “Cured”, an original work of speculative fiction by Dr Dána-Ain Davis. It opened the question this project keeps asking.

The project

Two lanes, one thread

The work moves in two directions at once.

Back, into the archive, to speculate through composition and collective storytelling about Anarcha’s life after she was called “cured”.

Forward, into the present, where Black and Brown people still face disproportionate maternal and infant death, in the UK and globally. Here the project develops a research programme alongside the communities living this, using Endarkened Co-Composition, and their stories shape the second part of the opera.

Underneath both lanes is a question about method: can arts-based approaches be a way of doing clinical and social research, not only of responding to it?

The name

Why One Long Thread

One long thread runs from Montgomery in the 1840s to the clinic, the maternity ward and the archive today. This research follows it in both directions.

It moves from and toward wholeness: listening again to these histories rather than treating the people in them as broken things to be repaired. A thread runs through, connects and holds: many voices, and the sound of them, held together.

Six threads are woven into it: testimony, healing, repair, embodied archives, dreaming, and the sonic.
